The decision

Verdict

Navy and gold. Redraw the mark in one colour.

Primary #123A6B. Achievement #FFC53D. Ground #0A1B33. Green and red stay free for outcomes. Gold appears on exactly one thing — the rating — and is never borrowed for a button.

Every one of the eleven colour pairings this app actually renders passes WCAG AA at normal text size. Not "mostly", not "at large sizes" — 11 of 11.

The one thing gold cannot do

Gold #FFC53D on white is 1.58 : 1 — invisible. Any light surface showing a rating must use #8A5A00 instead. This is the single most likely mistake when applying the palette, because the colour looks fine in a swatch and disappears in use.

On the mark

The two figures and ball is a good silhouette that reads at small sizes. The gradient is the weak part: it goes muddy at a 48px launcher icon, forces a light background, and is the reason the app and its own logo currently use different colours. Redrawn as solid gold on a navy tile, it survives the launcher, matches the product, and gains the trophy association the rating already depends on.

Why anyone comes back

Retention is a design problem, not a notification problem

A scouting app has a brutal retention shape: a player uploads once, then has nothing to do until something happens to them. If nothing does, they leave and never come back. Every design decision below exists to close that gap.

The four returns, and what each depends on

The rule that makes the difference

Never send a hollow notification. A weekly digest that says "0 views, no change" is a weekly reminder that nobody is looking, and it trains people to swipe the app away unread. Silence on a quiet week is what makes the loud weeks mean something.

Three design decisions that do more for retention than any push

1. The rating must look like an object worth owning

This is why gold on navy matters beyond aesthetics. A number in a blue panel is a statistic. The same number in a card with a metal edge is a possession — the visual language of a football card, which this audience already reads fluently. People screenshot possessions. They do not screenshot statistics. The card is the app's only organic growth mechanic.

2. Progress must be visible before it is impressive

A player rated 25 will never top a rating board and knows it. The improvement board is not a nice extra — it is the only place they can place, and it is why +7 is rendered in green at the same weight as the rating itself. What is measured as progress must be shown as progress.

3. The empty state is the retention surface

A new player has no rating, no views and no rank. That is the moment most of them decide. Every empty state must name the next action rather than the absence: "upload a highlight and any gain shows here" rather than "no data".

What none of this fixes

If a player uploads and no scout ever views them, no notification design saves the account. Retention mechanics amplify real activity; they cannot manufacture it. Filling one segment with enough scouts that a view is likely matters more than every screen in this document.

Part Three

The screens

Twenty-three screens in the proposed system, in the order a person meets them. Navy carries structure and action; gold appears only on a rating.

Arriving

1 · SplashHeld until fonts and session resolve. Navy in both themes — the first frame already teaches the palette.
2 · WelcomeLeads with the outcome, not the technology. The third option removes the wall in front of the value.
3 · OnboardingThree slides. Skippable, so nothing load-bearing lives only here.
4 · Browse (signed out)Real players before any ask. Adults only — under-18 profiles are excluded in the database.
5 · Sign upBoth documents open in-app, so reading them does not abandon registration.
6 · Profile completionOnly four fields are required. The minor notice appears the moment the date of birth says so.

Being a player

7 · Player homeThe card is the hero and the only gold on screen. "A scout viewed your profile" sits directly beneath it.
8 · Rating revealThe one orchestrated moment. Gold fills the badge here and nowhere else — this is the screenshot.
9 · AI ratingsThe screen that justifies the number. Confidence in words and colour, never colour alone.
10 · ReelsFull-bleed video, minimal chrome. The rating badge in gold is the only brand colour — everything else gets out of the way.
11 · UploadThe data cost is stated before the transfer. On metered data that is a courtesy that earns trust.
12 · LeaderboardImprovement is the default. Your own row is highlighted — the single change that makes a board feel personal.

Being scouted, and scouting

13 · Player detail (scout)The assessment screen. Footage sits above the attribute detail because watching decides, not reading.
14 · CompareThe bold green marks the winner — except where the winning value is low-confidence, where the app refuses to call it.
15 · Scout dashboardA working surface. The recommendation reason is real, drawn from their own filters — never invented.
16 · Scout searchThe rating badge is navy-and-gold at row scale, and the confidence dot rides on it so a soft number is visible while skimming.
17 · TrialsThe anti-fraud warning sits inline with the listings, not buried in Help. This is where the harm actually happens.
18 · MessagesThe safety notice is pinned above the inbox permanently — not a one-time dismissible tip.

Trust, control, and leaving

19 · Guardian consentThe only screen built for someone who never installs the app. Confirmation happens on the web, not here.
20 · NotificationsAll three retention triggers in one view. Each routes to what it is about; none is decorative.
21 · Notification settingsPer-category control and quiet hours. Giving people the off switch is what stops them uninstalling instead.
22 · SettingsThe guardian row appears only for under-18s, badged when consent is still outstanding.
23 · Log outSays explicitly that nothing is deleted — the confusion that makes people delete their account instead of signing out.

The honest bottom line

The engineering is stronger than the product. Access control is enforced in the database, no data is fabricated anywhere, confidence is surfaced rather than hidden, and the child-safety work is genuinely ahead of the market. None of that is visible to a user in the first ten seconds.

What is visible is a palette that disagrees with its own logo, a rating that looks like a statistic rather than something earned, and — until this week — a scout who could not press play. Fix the visible layer, then spend everything else on getting enough scouts into one region that a player's upload is actually seen.
