Read this first

The four findings that matter

If you read nothing else, read these. Two of them are uncomfortable.

1. The concept is not novel, and the AI is not the differentiator

Matobev is entering a market with at least six direct competitors, several better funded. Scout Me Pro is nearly identical in positioning — "TikTok for football", AI video analysis, computer-vision performance scores, players aged 13+ with parental-consent support, scouts with advanced filters, free to start. Afriskaut relaunched in 2025 at the Viareggio Cup and is in talks with the NBA. SOCALOCA has a partnership with the Ghana Football Association. ScoutMe, Scoutfutbol and Afriq Foot Scout all occupy the same sentence.

Every one of them claims AI analysis. Matobev's engine currently scores four attributes and is uncertain about all of them. As a differentiator, "we have AI" is already table stakes and Matobev is behind on it.

2. The real opening is trust, and it is enormous

The documented problem in African football is not that talent goes unseen. It is that tens of thousands of young players a year are defrauded and trafficked by people posing as agents. Families pay for trials that do not exist. Players arrive abroad, have documents and phones seized, and are forced to extract more money from home. This is reported by ISS Africa, ENACT, VICE and others, and the recommended remedies are consistently about verification and publishing who is genuine.

Where Matobev is genuinely ahead

Not one competitor positions on safety. Matobev already enforces guardian consent in the database, hides under-18 profiles from the open internet, enforces blocking in row-level security, and states in its terms that nobody may ever ask a player for money. That is a real position in a market defined by confident AI claims and a documented trafficking problem — and most of it is already built.

3. The honesty about uncertainty is the product's best idea

Every competitor sells a confident "performance score". Matobev marks confidence, mutes low-confidence values, and refuses to declare a winner in a comparison it cannot support. That is unusual, defensible, and impossible to copy without admitting your own numbers are soft.

It is also the harder sell. A scout skimming a store listing wants a number. Matobev's pitch has to be "the ratings you can trust because we tell you when not to" — which is a better long-term position and a worse screenshot.

4. It has not been used, and it shows

In one ninety-second review, three defects surfaced: a scout could not play a player's video at all, the back button was invisible on a light photo, and the leaderboard offered scouts a filter on a position scouts do not have. None are subtle. All three survived because the app was verified by typechecks and bundles rather than by a person using it as a scout.

Part One

What Matobev is

A talent-discovery platform for African footballers. A player uploads a highlight from their phone; software analyses how they move and produces a rating with a confidence attached; scouts search, watch, shortlist and make contact.

The problem

Discovery in African football runs on physical presence and personal networks — being at the right academy, knowing someone, being able to travel. Each is a filter on circumstance rather than ability. Into that gap steps a large, organised fraud industry selling the one thing families will pay for: hope.

The guardian is a genuine third user, not an afterthought. They never install the app — they receive a link. That is the only screen in the product built for someone who is not a user.

The two loops the product depends on

Player loop: upload → get rated → be seen → be told someone looked → upload again. It breaks if nothing ever comes back. That is why "a scout viewed your profile", "your rating went up" and the weekly summary exist, and why the improvement leaderboard is the default board — a rating-sorted list tells the bottom 90% they are nowhere.

Scout loop: filter → skim → watch → shortlist → contact. It breaks at watch if footage is slow, unplayable, or not worth the data. For a scout on mobile data, every video that turns out to be useless is money.

Part Two

What people will like, and what they will not

Written as bluntly as I can. The second column is the one worth arguing with.

Players

Will likeWill not
Being told a scout looked at them. This is the single most emotionally valuable thing the app does, and it is already built. A low rating. A 16 out of 99 on a scale they read fluently from FIFA reads as a verdict on them as a person. The confidence marker helps, but a teenager will remember the number.
Free, with nobody asking for money. In this market that is not a feature, it is a relief. Uploading a video on mobile data and getting nothing back. The upload is the single most expensive action in the app, in real money.
The improvement board. A category they can actually top. Being asked for a guardian. Correct, necessary, and a real drop-off point. Some will lie about their age instead.
The rating reveal moment. The one orchestrated moment; it is what gets screenshotted and shared. Waiting. Analysis is not instant, and "processing" with no estimate feels broken on a slow connection.

Scouts

Will likeWill not
Filters that match how they think — position, age band, region, minimum rating. Ratings they cannot interrogate. A scout will not trust a number without knowing what produced it. The breakdown exists; it must be one tap away, not three.
Notes and folders. Unglamorous and the reason a scout returns. Thin inventory. A scouting app with 40 players is not worth opening twice. This is the hardest problem the product has.
Confidence markers — once explained. It tells them which numbers to ignore, which saves journeys. Verification friction before they can see anything of value.
The thing nobody will say out loud

The cold-start problem is the real risk, not the design. Players join because scouts are there; scouts join because players are there. Every competitor listed in this document is fighting the same fight, and two of them have federation partnerships. A design document cannot solve this. Seeding one region, one age group, or one academy relationship properly matters more than any screen in Part Four.

Part Four

Design direction

Argued from what the product is trying to be, not from what the logo currently happens to be.

Correcting my earlier advice

An earlier version of this document built its whole colour argument on "the logo is the one asset that will not change". I assumed that; it was never established. If the logo can change — and it can — then the right question is not what matches the mark but what should this product look like, and the mark follows.

What the visual identity has to do

  1. Read as trustworthy, not as hype. The competition is dark, neon and TikTok-styled. In a category where the adjacent industry is fraud, looking like a growth-hacked crypto app is an active liability.
  2. Be legible in sunlight on a cheap screen. Higher contrast than a design-system default, larger body text, no thin weights.
  3. Make the rating feel earned but not final. It must feel worth having and must not read as a verdict.
  4. Survive being one of nine icons in a crowded store listing of blue football apps.

Two credible directions

DirectionArgument forArgument againstVerdict
Pitch green + gold
#0B6B3A · #FFC53D		 Football's own colour. Instantly legible in a store listing. Warm, and differentiates hard from a market of dark blue apps. Strong resonance across African flags. Green already means "success" in every interface convention. Using it for primary actions collides with that meaning, and this app needs a clean success state for applications and consent. Distinctive, but it spends a semantic colour the product needs
Deep navy + gold
#123A6B · #FFC53D		 Institutional and calm — the register of a federation, not a startup. Leaves green and red free for outcomes. Gold on navy is the strongest pairing available at 10.93 : 1. Navy is the most crowded palette in app stores. Without a distinctive mark it disappears. Recommended, on the condition that the logo carries the distinctiveness

Recommendation, and what it means for the logo

Deep navy as the ground, gold as the single accent, and the logo redrawn in gold on navy. The reasoning:

  • Navy is the colour of institutions people trust with children. That is the exact register a platform positioned on safety should occupy.
  • Gold means achievement and nothing else — the rating, a rank, a milestone. One colour, one meaning, never borrowed for a button.
  • It leaves green, red and amber entirely free for outcomes, which a product with applications, consent states and moderation genuinely needs.
  • The current teal is not wrong, but it does two jobs at once — brand and primary action — and it is the reason the app and the mark currently disagree.

On the mark itself: the two figures and ball is a good silhouette and works small. The gradient is the weak part — it renders muddy at icon sizes and forces a light background. A single-colour version, gold on navy, would survive a 48px launcher icon far better and finally give the app and its logo the same palette.
